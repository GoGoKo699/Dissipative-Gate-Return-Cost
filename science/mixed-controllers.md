# Exactly returned mixed controllers

A finite mixed controller cannot remove the inverse-error action cost when its marginal is returned exactly. The same conclusion already follows from return on one particular input: the maximally mixed data state. With the generator action defined below, the bound is

```math
(s(U)-\epsilon)_+^2\le A\epsilon.
```

It is independent of controller dimension and of the nonzero eigenvalues of its initial state. An explicit two-qubit construction attains the inverse-action order for a $`Z`$ gate while returning a maximally mixed controller marginal exactly at every time.

The return promise here permits correlations with the data. It differs from approximate **joint** return of a pure controller. The [consumed-controller note](consumed-controllers.md) compares these promises with a construction that changes the supplied pure controller.

## Model, action, and error

Let $`S`$ be a finite data system of dimension $`d`$, and let $`C`$ be any finite controller with initial density operator $`\beta`$. The initial state is $`\rho\otimes\beta`$. The joint dynamics is deterministic, unmonitored, and generated by predetermined Hermitian Lindblad operators:

```math
\frac{d\sigma_t}{dt}=\mathcal L_t(\sigma_t),\qquad
\mathcal L_t(X)
=\sum_j\left(L_j(t)XL_j(t)-\frac12\{L_j(t)^2,X\}\right)
=-\frac12\sum_j[L_j(t),[L_j(t),X]],
\qquad L_j(t)=L_j(t)^\dagger.
```

There is no Hamiltonian term. Piecewise continuous controls suffice; the arguments also apply to integrable controls whenever the corresponding propagator and identities are well defined. All supplied controller degrees of freedom are included in $`C`$. An external data reference evolves trivially.

Write $`\|X\|_2^2=\operatorname{Tr}(X^\dagger X)`$ and define

```math
\|\mathcal L_t\|_{2\to2}
:=\sup_{X\ne0}\frac{\|\mathcal L_t(X)\|_2}{\|X\|_2},
\qquad
\alpha(t):=\frac12\|\mathcal L_t\|_{2\to2},
\qquad
A:=\int_0^T\alpha(t)\,dt.
```

This is the induced operator norm on Hilbert–Schmidt space, not the Frobenius norm of a superoperator matrix. It depends on the generator itself and is unchanged by rewriting a Hermitian-jump representation. It obeys

```math
A\le\int_0^T\sum_j\|L_j(t)\|_\infty^2\,dt,
```

because $`\|[L_j,\cdot]\|_{2\to2}\le2\|L_j\|_\infty`$. Thus every lower bound in terms of $`A`$ also gives a lower bound for the displayed sum-of-jump-norm budget. Tensoring with an inert reference does not change $`\|\mathcal L_t\|_{2\to2}`$.

Let $`\Phi_T`$ be the joint propagator, and let

```math
\mathcal E(\rho):=\operatorname{Tr}_C\Phi_T(\rho\otimes\beta),
\qquad
\mathcal U(\rho):=U\rho U^\dagger.
```

The standard complete-channel error is $`\epsilon=\tfrac12\|\mathcal E-\mathcal U\|_\diamond`$. For the converse it is enough to assume the weaker pure-input estimate

```math
\frac12\|\mathcal E(\rho)-U\rho U^\dagger\|_1\le\epsilon
\quad\text{for every pure state }\rho\text{ on }S.
```

The sole return hypothesis used in the proof is

```math
\operatorname{Tr}_S\Phi_T\left(\frac{I_S}{d}\otimes\beta\right)=\beta.
\tag{R}
```

Exact controller marginal return for every data input implies (R). Neither exact joint product return nor preservation of a purification of $`\beta`$ is assumed.

## 1. Return on the maximally mixed input forces a stationary product

Set $`\tau=I_S/d\otimes\beta`$, and evolve this particular initial state to $`\omega_t=\Phi_t(\tau)`$. Condition (R) gives $`\operatorname{Tr}_S(\omega_T-\tau)=0`$. Consequently the Hilbert–Schmidt cross term vanishes and

```math
\operatorname{Tr}(\omega_T^2)
=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\beta^2)}{d}
 +\|\omega_T-\tau\|_2^2.
\tag{1}
```

For Hermitian jumps, purity has the exact derivative

```math
\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Tr}(\omega_t^2)
=-\sum_j\|[L_j(t),\omega_t]\|_2^2\le0.
\tag{2}
```

The first term on the right of (1) is the initial purity. Hence (1) and (2) force equality: $`\omega_T=\tau`$ and the integrated nonnegative quantity in (2) is zero. Every commutator in (2) vanishes almost everywhere, the derivative of $`\omega_t`$ is zero almost everywhere, and therefore

```math
\omega_t=\tau\quad(0\le t\le T),
\qquad
[L_j(t),I_S\otimes\beta]=0
\quad\text{for every }j\text{ almost everywhere}.
\tag{3}
```

This is a trajectory statement. A final marginal condition has forced every instantaneous Hermitian jump to preserve the spectral blocks of the initial controller state.

### Singular controller states

No full-rank assumption was used in (1)–(3). Let $`P_C`$ be the support projector of $`\beta`$. Since it is a spectral projector of $`\beta`$, equation (3) implies $`[L_j,I_S\otimes P_C]=0`$. The support of every allowed initial state $`\rho\otimes\beta`$ is therefore invariant. In the following argument, restrict to that support and use $`\beta^{-1}`$ there. Equivalently, use the support inverse with all operators restricted to the supported subspace. Arbitrarily small positive eigenvalues cause no dimension- or condition-number factor in the final result.

For a pure controller, this argument fixes the subspace $`S\otimes|0\rangle`$ throughout the evolution. In particular, an exact pure-return promise excludes the subspace excursions used by the approximate joint-return construction. No matching exact-pure-return construction is inferred from that approximate construction.

## 2. A weighted speed–purity inequality

On the invariant support define the Hilbert-space inner product and norm

```math
\langle X,Y\rangle_\beta
:=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[X^\dagger(I_S\otimes\beta^{-1})Y\right],
\qquad
\|X\|_\beta^2:=\langle X,X\rangle_\beta,
\qquad
P_\beta(t):=\|\sigma_t\|_\beta^2.
```

The map $`J(X)=(I_S\otimes\beta^{-1/2})X`$ is an isometry from this weighted space to ordinary Hilbert–Schmidt space on the support. Equation (3) gives $`J\mathcal L_t=\mathcal L_tJ`$. Thus $`K_t=-\mathcal L_t`$ is positive and self-adjoint in the weighted inner product, and its weighted induced norm is the ordinary induced norm of the restricted generator. That norm is at most the full-space $`\|\mathcal L_t\|_{2\to2}`$.

It follows that

```math
-P_\beta'(t)
=2\langle\sigma_t,K_t\sigma_t\rangle_\beta
=\sum_j\|[L_j(t),\sigma_t]\|_\beta^2,
```

and, using $`K_t^2\le\|K_t\|K_t`$,

```math
\|\sigma_t'\|_\beta^2
=\langle\sigma_t,K_t^2\sigma_t\rangle_\beta
\le \frac12\|\mathcal L_t\|_{2\to2}\,[-P_\beta'(t)]
=\alpha(t)[-P_\beta'(t)].
\tag{4}
```

The triangle inequality for the integral and Cauchy–Schwarz in time now give

```math
\|\sigma_T-\sigma_0\|_\beta^2
\le\left(\int_0^T\sqrt{\alpha(t)[-P_\beta'(t)]}\,dt\right)^2
\le A\,[P_\beta(0)-P_\beta(T)].
\tag{5}
```

The weight is needed because ordinary joint purity would scale with $`\operatorname{Tr}\beta^2`$. In (5), a pure data state initially has weighted purity one regardless of the controller spectrum.

## 3. Projection to the data and the finite-action converse

The map

```math
\Pi_\beta(X):=\operatorname{Tr}_C(X)\otimes\beta
```

is an orthogonal projection for $`\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\beta`$. Indeed, for every data operator $`Z`$,

```math
\langle Z\otimes\beta,X-\Pi_\beta(X)\rangle_\beta=0,
\qquad
\|Z\otimes\beta\|_\beta=\|Z\|_2.
```

Therefore

```math
\|\operatorname{Tr}_C X\|_2\le\|X\|_\beta,
\qquad
P_\beta(T)\ge\operatorname{Tr}[\mathcal E(\rho)^2].
\tag{6}
```

Choose a pure $`\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|`$, write $`\rho_{\rm out}=\mathcal E(\rho)`$ and $`\varphi=U\rho U^\dagger`$, and let $`p=\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi\rho_{\rm out})`$. The assumed error gives $`p\ge1-\epsilon`$. Equations (5) and (6), together with $`P_\beta(0)=1`$, imply

```math
\|\rho_{\rm out}-\rho\|_2^2
\le A[1-P_\beta(T)]
\le A[1-p^2]
\le 2A\epsilon.
\tag{7}
```

For a traceless Hermitian operator $`X`$, $`\|X\|_2\le\|X\|_1/\sqrt2`$: the positive and negative eigenvalues have equal total magnitude, and the sum of squares in each part is no greater than the square of that magnitude. In particular,

```math
\frac{\|\rho_{\rm out}-\varphi\|_2}{\sqrt2}\le\epsilon.
```

Define the pure-input target displacement

```math
s_\psi(U)
:=\frac{\|U\rho U^\dagger-\rho\|_2}{\sqrt2}
=\sqrt{1-|\langle\psi|U|\psi\rangle|^2}.
```

Using the triangle inequality in (7) yields

```math
s_\psi(U)\le\sqrt{A\epsilon}+\epsilon,
\qquad
(s_\psi(U)-\epsilon)_+^2\le A\epsilon.
\tag{8}
```

### Why an unentangled pure input suffices for the target displacement

For a unitary channel and the identity, define

```math
s(U):=\frac12\|\mathcal U-\mathrm{id}\|_\diamond.
```

A reference-assisted pure input has overlap $`\operatorname{Tr}(\eta U)`$ with its image, where $`\eta`$ is its reduced data state. Since $`U`$ is normal, these overlaps form the convex hull of its eigenvalues. Every convex combination $`\sum_k p_k e^{i\theta_k}`$ is also realized by the unentangled vector $`\sum_k\sqrt{p_k}|u_k\rangle`$. Hence

```math
s(U)=\max_{|\psi\rangle}s_\psi(U)
=\sqrt{1-\min_\eta|\operatorname{Tr}(\eta U)|^2}.
```

A pure data input therefore achieves the required displacement even though $`s(U)`$ is a complete-channel distance. Maximizing (8) proves

```math
\boxed{(s(U)-\epsilon)_+^2\le A\epsilon.}
\tag{9}
```

For a nontrivial unitary channel, $`s(U)>0`$, and the equivalent lower bound is

```math
\epsilon\ge
\frac{A+2s(U)-\sqrt{A^2+4As(U)}}{2}
=\frac{2s(U)^2}{A+2s(U)+\sqrt{A^2+4As(U)}}.
\tag{10}
```

In particular, $`\epsilon=\Omega_U(A^{-1})`$, or $`A=\Omega_U(\epsilon^{-1})`$ at small error. These are order statements; no optimal universal prefactor is asserted.

## 4. Initially maximally mixed controllers may be discarded and still obey the bound

If $`\beta=I_C/D`$, the reference input in (R) is the global maximally mixed state. Every Hermitian-jump generator is unital, so this reference state is stationary automatically. No final return requirement is then needed to invoke (9).

Thus an initially maximally mixed controller obeys (9) even if its actual output on other inputs is discarded. The exponential construction in [consumed-controllers.md](consumed-controllers.md) uses an initially pure controller and changes its marginal. Its advantage cannot be attributed to taking a partial trace alone: access to consumable nonuniformity of the initial controller is essential to that construction.

For a general nonuniform $`\beta`$, an approximate marginal-return promise does not imply (3). This proof establishes no dimension-uniform extension to arbitrary approximately returned mixed controllers.

## 5. An exact mixed-return $`Z`$ gate with inverse-action error

Let both $`S`$ and $`C`$ be qubits, put $`\beta=I_C/2`$, and take the single Hermitian jump

```math
L(t)=\sqrt\gamma\left[
 |0\rangle\langle0|_S\otimes Z_C
 +|1\rangle\langle1|_S\otimes
 \left(\sin\frac{2\pi t}{T}\,X_C+\cos\frac{2\pi t}{T}\,Z_C\right)
 \right],\qquad 0\le t\le T.
\tag{11}
```

Here $`L(t)^2=\gamma I`$, and $`\mathcal L_t`$ has Hilbert–Schmidt eigenvalues zero and $`-2\gamma`$. Therefore its action is exactly $`A=\gamma T`$. The schedule and jump are prescribed; no controller measurement, feedback, or physical change-of-frame Hamiltonian is applied.

The two diagonal data blocks each evolve the maximally mixed controller by a unital channel and keep it fixed. Consequently the controller marginal is exactly $`I_C/2`$ at every time for every data input, including data initially entangled with an inert reference. Correlations between data and controller remain permitted.

### Exact two-dimensional equation

Use normalized time $`u=t/T`$, and let $`B(u)`$ be the controller operator multiplying the initial data off-diagonal block. It obeys

```math
B'(u)=A\,[Z_C B(u)(\boldsymbol n(u)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_C)-B(u)],
\qquad B(0)=I_C/2,
\qquad\boldsymbol n(u)=(\sin 2\pi u,0,\cos 2\pi u).
```

Let $`R(u)=e^{-i\pi uY_C}`$, so $`\boldsymbol n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma=RZR^\dagger`$. The change of variables $`C(u)=B(u)R(u)`$ is an algebraic device. Its exact equation is

```math
C'=A(ZCZ-C)-i\pi CY.
```

Writing $`C(u)=[a(u)I+b(u)iY]/2`$ reduces this to the real system

```math
\begin{pmatrix}a'\\b'\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&\pi\\-\pi&-2A\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a(0)=1,\quad b(0)=0.
\tag{12}
```

Equivalently, $`a''+2Aa'+\pi^2a=0`$, with $`a'(0)=0`$. Since $`R(1)=-I`$, the final coherence multiplier is $`\operatorname{Tr}B(1)=-a(1)`$. Hence the complete reduced data channel is

```math
\mathcal E_A
=\frac{1-a(1)}2\,\mathrm{id}
 +\frac{1+a(1)}2\,\mathcal Z,
\qquad \mathcal Z(\rho)=Z\rho Z,
```

and its half-diamond error is exactly

```math
\epsilon_A=\frac12\|\mathcal E_A-\mathcal Z\|_\diamond
=\frac{1-a(1)}2.
\tag{13}
```

For $`A>\pi`$, with $`\Delta=\sqrt{A^2-\pi^2}`$,

```math
a(1)=e^{-A}\left(\cosh\Delta+\frac{A}{\Delta}\sinh\Delta\right).
\tag{14}
```

At $`A=\pi`$ its continuous value is $`e^{-\pi}(1+\pi)`$. For $`0\le A<\pi`$, let $`\delta=\sqrt{\pi^2-A^2}`$ and replace the bracket in (14) by $`\cos\delta+(A/\delta)\sin\delta`$. At zero action, $`a(1)=-1`$ and the actual channel is the identity, as required.

### Finite-action estimate and asymptotics

Equation (12) gives the dissipative identity

```math
\frac{d}{du}(a^2+b^2)=-4Ab^2\le0,
```

so $`|a(u)|\le1`$. Solving the equation for $`b`$ and integrating the equation for $`a`$ gives

```math
b(u)=-\pi\int_0^u e^{-2A(u-v)}a(v)\,dv,
```

```math
1-a(1)
=\pi^2\int_0^1du\int_0^u dv\,e^{-2A(u-v)}a(v)
\le\frac{\pi^2}{2A}-\frac{\pi^2(1-e^{-2A})}{4A^2}
\quad(A>0).
```

In particular,

```math
0\le\epsilon_A\le\min\left\{1,\frac{\pi^2}{4A}\right\},
\qquad
\epsilon_A=\frac{\pi^2}{4A}+O(A^{-2})\quad(A\to\infty).
\tag{15}
```

The target has $`s(Z)=1`$, witnessed by $`|+\rangle`$. Combining (9) and (15) proves the optimal inverse-action **order** for this fixed nontrivial gate among all finite controllers with exact marginal return. Equation (11) establishes an upper bound for $`Z`$; it does not establish an exactly returned mixed-controller construction for every unitary.

## Return, correlations, and structural prior

Exact marginal return means the controller's local density operator is restored. It does not require the joint data–controller state to factor, does not restore an arbitrary external purification of the controller, and does not by itself supply an independent fresh controller for an arbitrary sequence of future tasks. Those are distinct operational promises.

Lie and Jeong's *Catalytic quantum randomness as a correlational resource*, Physical Review Research **3**, 043089 (2021), is a structural predecessor. Proposition 5 and the proof of Theorem 8 concern compatible unitary catalytic transformations, maximally mixed inputs, and the catalyst's spectral structure. The paper also makes explicit the role of consumed uncorrelatedness in marginally returned catalysts. The additional argument here is the stationary-product implication for a Hermitian dissipative trajectory, followed by the weighted, generator-action inequality (4)–(9). The unitary-catalysis structure itself is not claimed as new. [Primary article and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.3.043089).

The action $`A`$ measures the strength and duration of the permitted generator. It does not include a derivation of the engineered bath coupling, a gate-compilation cost, or thermodynamic work. In (11) the coupling and its rotation are explicit fixed-size controls. Adding Hamiltonian control, monitored outcomes with feedback, or uncounted reset resources changes the model to which the converse applies.
